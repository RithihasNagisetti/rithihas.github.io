Getting Started: First Week as a PhD Student

May 4, 2026 · Nagisetti Rithihas

Starting a PhD is something I had been working toward for a long time — through five years at IIT Kharagpur, two summers of research internships, and a master's thesis that pushed me deep into 3D computer vision. Now that the first week at WashU is done, I wanted to write something down before the feeling fades.

The Lab

I'm joining the Computational Medical Imaging and Technology (CMIT) Lab under Dr. Abhinav K. Jha. The lab works at the intersection of imaging physics, statistical methods, and deep learning — applied to nuclear medicine and SPECT imaging. The questions here are different from anything I worked on before: not just "does the model perform well?" but "can we trust what the model tells us about a patient?"

What I'm Thinking About

My interests going in are generative AI for medical imaging, hallucination analysis in deep networks, and what the manifold hypothesis can tell us about how learned representations behave in high-dimensional imaging data. The SNMMI Student Research Grant I received this year will fund work on patient-specific MPI SPECT reconstruction using deep learning — a good concrete anchor while the bigger questions take shape.

What's Next

For now: reading papers, getting familiar with the SPECT pipeline, and figuring out which open problems are worth the next four years. I'll use this blog to share things I learn along the way — research notes, paper summaries, and the occasional honest reflection on what PhD life actually looks like.
