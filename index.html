Nagisetti Rithihas

PhD Student — Imaging Science

McKelvey School of Engineering, Washington University in St. Louis
CMIT Lab · Advisor: Dr. Abhinav K. Jha

I am a first-year PhD student in Imaging Science at Washington University in St. Louis, working in the Computational Medical Imaging and Technology (CMIT) Lab. My research interests lie in generative AI for medical imaging, high-dimensional statistical inference, and the manifold hypothesis as a framework for understanding learned representations in deep networks. I am particularly interested in hallucination analysis and explainability/interpretability of black-box AI models in safety-critical clinical settings, with current applications in SPECT domain imaging.

I completed a dual degree in Electronics & Communication Engineering (B.Tech) and Vision & Intelligent Systems (M.Tech) at IIT Kharagpur. My prior work spans 3D human pose analysis, temporal NeRF-based 3D reconstruction & generation, combat sports video analytics, and cardiac signal processing.

Selected Publications

CVCI

Efficient Boxing Punch Classification: Fine-Grained Skeleton-based Recognition Made Light

Vipul Baghel, Nagisetti Rithihas, Babji Srinivasan, Ravi Sadananda Hegde

Computer Vision and Computational Intelligence (CVCI), 2024

We present a lightweight skeleton-based approach for fine-grained boxing punch classification. Leveraging pose-to-pose graph representations and angular encoding, our method achieves high accuracy while remaining computationally efficient for real-time sports analytics applications.

CVIP

A Novel Hierarchical Pipeline for Fine-Grained Punch Recognition in Uncontrolled Settings

Vipul Baghel, Sagar Deep Deb, Nagisetti Rithihas, Babji Srinivasan, Ravi Sadananda Hegde

International Conference on Computer Vision & Image Processing (CVIP), 2024 — Accepted

We propose a hierarchical pipeline for recognizing boxing punches in wild-setting videos where lighting, camera angle, and occlusion vary significantly. Using 3D pose estimation and per-frame probability regression, the system detects and classifies punch instances with 96.09% accuracy.
